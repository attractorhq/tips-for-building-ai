# Resources

This is a deliberately short reading list. Prefer the source closest to the
decision you need to make, and verify that guidance still applies to your
system and jurisdiction.

## Risk and governance

- [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework)
  provides a vendor-neutral structure for governing, mapping, measuring, and
  managing AI risk.
- [NIST Generative AI Profile](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1) applies
  that framework to risks specific to generative AI.

## Security

- [OWASP Top 10 for Large Language Model Applications](https://genai.owasp.org/llm-top-10/)
  is a practical starting point for system-level threat modelling.
- [MITRE ATLAS](https://atlas.mitre.org/) documents adversarial tactics and
  techniques observed in AI-enabled systems.

## Reliability and operations

- [Google's Site Reliability Engineering books](https://sre.google/books/)
  are freely available references for service levels, monitoring, incident
  response, and operational ownership.
- [The Incident Database](https://incidentdatabase.ai/) contains public cases
  that can help teams think beyond their own happy paths.

## Evaluation and transparency

- [NIST's ARIA program](https://ai-challenges.nist.gov/aria)
  describes evaluation across model testing, red-teaming, and field testing.
- [Model Cards for Model Reporting](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03993)
  introduced a useful pattern for recording intended use, evaluation, and
  limitations.

## Using references well

Standards and lists do not replace product judgment. Connect each reference to
a concrete risk, decision, control, owner, and piece of evidence in your own
system.
